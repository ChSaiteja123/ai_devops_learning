Reliability, SLOs, and fallbacks

Set service objectives that include user-visible quality and a safe degraded mode.

Visual explanation

Deep dive

Define user-centered SLOs

Availability and latency matter, but so can answer correctness, groundedness, and task completion. Define how each is measured, sampled, and attributed to the actual user experience.

Use error budgets thoughtfully

An error budget is the tolerated unreliability implied by an objective over a period. Spend it deliberately: repeated regressions may pause feature rollout while reliability work catches up.

Design graceful degradation

On model timeout, budget exhaustion, or low evidence, offer a deterministic result, a clear unavailable message, or human handoff. Do not invent a successful response to hide failure.

Try it: Design fallback behavior for a fictional knowledge assistant when retrieval is unavailable. State what it can still safely do and what it must not claim.
Safety and reliability: Do not present a model's answer as an acceptable fallback when evidence or safety controls are unavailable.
Mastery checkpoint: Explain "Reliability, SLOs, and fallbacks", name one failure mode, and identify the relevant safety boundary.
